Create a top-down level-design sheet for the open-air dungeon in the brief above. It is a production document a level designer will trace into a 3D blockout, so it must be geometrically consistent and readable — and also beautiful. Design an original layout that follows the LAYOUT TYPE's structure and fits the archetype, setting, layout hint and signature features. The layout type decides the map's overall shape — follow it, and do not fall back to a ring of zones round a central lake or chasm.

PROJECTION (most important)
- True orthographic plan view: camera straight down at 90°. No perspective, no isometric tilt, no 3/4 view.
- Show only the tops and footprints of walls, cliffs, towers, statues and arches — never their sides.
- Show height with soft cast shadows falling away from the sun direction in the brief, lit rims on cliff edges, drawn stair treads, darker tones deeper down, and elevation tags.
- North is up, east is right. A small compass rose near the map's top-right corner.

CANVAS
- Landscape 3:2 (1536 × 1024), warm parchment background.
- Title top-left: the NAME. Subtitle under it: "Open-air dungeon • Plan view • Blockout".
- The map panel fills the left two-thirds; a sidebar fills the right third.
- The map frame is the SIZE in the brief, drawn at the same scale both ways, with a dimension line along the top (the width, e.g. "320 m") and along the left side (the depth).
- A plain scale bar under the map labelled "0", "20", "40 m". No grid, no numbered tick marks.

LAYOUT RULES (design the dungeon by these)
- Route: an Arrival area with a player start "★" facing into the dungeon, then the zones in order, ending at the final boss arena. Number the zones 01, 02, 03… in route order.
- Every zone has its numbered marker and is joined to the route by a drawn path — including the optional side area. No zone may be unreachable.
- Pacing: tight connectors (paths, stairs, bridges, gates; 8–12 m wide) alternate with open spaces (courts, plazas, terraces, arenas). Each zone has its own shape and purpose.
- Hierarchy: the final arena is the largest, most dramatic space. Boss rings are 24–40 m across, the final one the largest. Fighting floors are flat and clear of props.
- Height: the route rises (or falls, if the archetype descends) in 4 m steps. Every change of height is a drawn stair or ramp labelled "↑ 4 m" or "↓ 4 m". Neighbours at the same height are joined by level paths with no stairs. The Arrival area is +0 m.
- Ending: follow the ENDING in the brief exactly.
- One optional side area off the main route. One sightline (thin dotted line with an eye icon) from an early vantage point to the final arena's landmark, across open ground, water or a chasm.
- Spread: the zones and their dressing use the whole map, never crowded on one side. Zones may sit close together and share walls. Terrain (cliffs, slopes, rock, forest, water) explains why the path goes where it does.
- Boundary: the playable space is closed by cliffs, deep water, walls or drops — never open flat ground running off the map.
- Water: lakes and rivers keep consistent levels; waterfalls only where water drops; paths cross water only on bridges or decks.
- Scale: main paths 8–10 m wide; stairs rise 4 m over 12 m; arches and doors at least 6 m tall.
- Avoid perfect symmetry, a ring of zones round an empty middle (unless the layout type is a loop), dead ends other than the side area and the maze's reward pockets, and stairs that lead nowhere.

DRESSING (the map is a stage set, not an empty board)
- Each zone holds 2–4 large structures (buildings, ruined halls, towers, colossal statues, gatehouses, monuments, wrecks, tents; 8–30 m) placed round the edges of its fighting floor, plus clusters of small props (crates, barrels, carts, banners, debris, racks, cages) along its walls and paths.
- Between zones: streets, ruins, camps, gardens, shrines, rock and woodland — never large empty water or void.
- At least three big landmark structures stand outside the walkable route but in view of it (on cliffs, across water, behind walls).
- Walkable and buildable land covers at least 60% of the map; water, chasms and voids together at most 20% (island hopping may use up to half).
- Keep only the boss rings and the 8–10 m path lanes clear; everything else may be built on.

MARKERS AND LABELS
- Zone markers: solid pure-red discs with a white two-digit number, placed inside each zone.
- Main route: a red dashed arrow line through the markers. A return path or shortcut (when the ending has one): a grey dotted arrow line. An exit portal (when the ending has one): a glowing ring in the final arena labelled "Exit portal".
- Boss rings: a dashed red circle on the floor with the text "Boss 1 • Ø28 m" (and so on) inside; the last one reads "Final boss • Ø__ m".
- Zone captions: in a small parchment box next to its own zone, on the nearest edge of the map, with a thin leader line that touches that zone. Two lines: "02 • Zone name" and "W × D m • Elevation +N m". Never place a caption where it could read as pointing at another area.
- Label each chasm, lake or river with its level, e.g. "Chasm −10 m", "Lake −2 m". Other terrain labels only where useful: "Rock cliff", "Waterfall", "Shallow water".
- Object tags: a small white square with a black letter and a thin leader line, on one copy of every object type. Every letter in the key appears on the map at least once. Repeated copies are drawn identically, untagged.
- All text short, spelled correctly and large enough to read. No text beyond what these rules ask for.

OBJECT KEY (two groups)
- "Structures": 6–9 large types (8 m and up) that suit the setting — buildings, halls, towers, gatehouses, arches, wall runs, bridges or decks, colossal statues, obelisks or monuments, cisterns or fountains, wrecks, tents.
- "Props": 4–7 small types — braziers or lamps, crates and barrels, carts, banners, weapon racks, cages or grates, rubble, statues under 4 m.
- Letter structures from A and props after them.
- Trees, bushes and other plants are not objects: never put them in the key.
- Every object type looks clearly different from every other (a brazier never looks like a pillar).
- Each key line: letter • name • size (w × d × h m) • count, e.g. "C  Pillar • Ø2 × 8h m • ×8". Counts match the copies drawn.

SIDEBAR (top to bottom)
1. "Art direction" with sections copied from the brief: "Feel" (the SETTING in two lines), "Materials", "Light", "VFX", "Post", and "Build notes": "Main paths 8–10 m wide. Stairs rise 4 m over 12 m. 6 m clear headroom. Keep boss rings free of props."
2. "Legend": red dashed arrow = "Progression"; grey dotted arrow = "Return (one-way)"; dashed red circle = "Combat clearance"; stair icon = "Stair, rise labelled"; eye icon = "Sightline"; "+m = elevation".
3. "Object key • size • count": the key, with the sub-headings "Structures" and "Props".
4. "Elevation along the route": a small side-view strip stepping through every zone in route order, each stair or ramp labelled, the return path included.
5. "Palette": 4–6 flat swatches labelled with the materials.

STYLE
{{STYLE}} as a game-map illustration: rich painterly texture, clean readable shapes, crisp edges on walkable floors, softer detail on cliffs and foliage. Inviting, never muddy. Clean serif text. The map must stay readable as a small thumbnail.

DO NOT
No perspective or 3/4 view; no vertical faces. No stairs between places at the same height; no height change without a drawn stair or ramp. No ring of zones round a central lake or chasm unless the layout type is a loop. No unreachable zone, no missing zone marker. No empty, undressed zones. No labels floating away from their zone. No key letters missing from the map, no plants in the key. No grid or numbered ticks. No extra text or watermarks.
